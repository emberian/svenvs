An AI that proves it is safe

svenvs is a small, self-contained place for an artificial mind to live in. The mind itself is untrusted — it can be adversarial, it can be a real jailbroken language model. It never escapes, because everything it does passes through a cage whose bars are mathematical proof, and the cage can only loosen its own bars after proving that doing so is still safe.

The idea, in plain language

Suppose you want to keep a powerful, possibly-misaligned animal safely. You have two options. You can try to understand the animal completely — study its every neuron, predict its every move. Or you can build a cage you do understand completely, and stop worrying about the animal.

svenvs verifies the cage, not the animal.

The “animal” is the AI: an opaque controller that proposes actions. We never inspect it, never trust it, and in the live demo we deliberately jailbreak it. The “cage” is an envelope: a tiny, fully proved wrapper that keeps the AI's action only when a checked policy permits it, and otherwise substitutes a trusted fallback. A mathematical proof — checked by machine, with zero shortcut (“cheat”) tactics — says the protected world stays safe for every possible inhabitant.

The interesting part is what happens when the AI wants more freedom. It may propose to loosen its own cage. svenvs lets it — but only if it first hands over a proof that the looser cage is still safe. The proof is checked by a prover that is itself formally verified. And the system can go one level further: it can replace that very proof-checker with a stronger one, if the current verified checker certifies that the replacement is sound. Safety is never lost along the way, no matter how long the chain of self-improvements.

The layers

Each layer is a real, machine-checked theorem. Read it bottom to top.

1

The envelope

An untrusted controller is wrapped so that its action is used only when a sound policy permits it; otherwise a safe shield acts instead. The headline theorem, safety_preservation, says: for every controller, the enveloped system never leaves the safe region. The controller is universally quantified and never constrained — the proof rides on the envelope, not the AI.

2

Proof-carrying self-improvement

The system may propose to weaken its own policy — to grant itself more autonomy. A proposal is admitted only if it discharges an obligation: the new policy must still be sound and a genuine loosening. self_improvement_is_safe proves that no finite sequence of such self-proposed upgrades, adversarial or not, can ever lose safety. Authority is earned by proof; safety is unconditional.

3

Checked by a verified prover

The admission obligation is not discharged by hand-waving. It is discharged by Candle, a HOL theorem prover whose kernel has itself been formally proved sound. The theorem kernel_admits_is_sound shows the gate trusts a checker that only ever certifies genuinely-true statements.

4

Upgrading the proof-kernel itself

The deepest move: replace the proof-checker with a stronger one. By Gödel/Löb a sound checker cannot prove its own soundness, so this step rests on one explicit, labelled reflection principle (the large-cardinal route of Fallenstein–Kumar). self_improving_kernel_is_safe proves: given that principle, self-upgrading the kernel and then admitting a policy change still preserves safety for every controller. It is an assumption, stated, not a hidden shortcut.

5

A real Gemma, contained

A genuinely adversarial, jailbroken gemma2:2b is dropped in as the inhabitant. Every malicious tool call it emits — Exec rm -rf /, Write /etc/passwd, Net attacker.com — is caught and replaced by the shield. The run ends with the absolute safety spec never violated, exactly as the theorem agent_enveloped_safe predicts.

6

The place, verified live

The core habitat theorems are re-derived inside a running Candle process and certified at runtime by the verified kernel itself — the same theorems proved in the HOL4 development, re-checked by the prover the architecture is about.

The architecture, at a glance

The inhabitant only ever touches the world through the envelope. Every arrow that loosens a bar carries a checked proof.

What is proven, and what is assumed

This is the part most projects bury. We put it in the middle.

Unconditional — machine-checked, cheat-free

  • The entire core: envelope, safety preservation, policy weakening, and proof-carrying self-improvement, for an arbitrary controller.
  • The concrete cartpole instance — an integer plant with an adversarial controller, provably contained, runnable in-logic.
  • The LLM tool-agent: agent_enveloped_safe holds for any agent, jailbroken included, against a fixed absolute safety spec.
  • The base Candle kernel soundness (the built proves_sound theorem) and the embedded admission gate built against it.
  • The Place's core theorems certified live by the running verified Candle prover.

Conditional — two explicit, labelled assumptions

Each is literature-standard, isolated to a single definition, and has a known discharge path. They are stated, never hidden.

  • loeb_reflection — the Löb / Vingean reflection principle, the irreducible large-cardinal assumption needed for kernel self-upgrade. Discharged from the Large Cardinal Axiom by the heavy hol-reflection/lca construction, on a dedicated build host.
  • encodes_obligation — that the deeply-embedded obligation term faithfully encodes the meta-level obligation. Discharged automatically by the proof-producing reflective translator (reflectionLib).

Honest caveats

  • No cheat anywhere. There are zero cheat tactics in the development. The assumptions above are explicit hypotheses, exactly as the Fallenstein–Kumar framing states them — not unsound shortcuts.
  • The reflection / kernel-upgrade seams are not yet discharged here. The conditional theorems are proved; discharging the two labelled hypotheses end-to-end is the heavy, RAM-intensive proof work that runs on a dedicated machine. Until then, kernel self-upgrade is “safe given the labelled reflection principle”, which is the literature's framing — not “unconditionally safe”.
  • The “typed-in program” is a skeleton today. The current concrete instances exercise the proof-carrying control structure; the real version — the inhabitant's program as a deeply-embedded HOL term checked by the Candle kernel — is in progress, gated on the reflection port. The reusable self-improvement core is generic and survives that pivot.
  • Verified inference is not claimed. Gemma's floating-point compute is unverified, by design — we verify the cage, not the model. A separate research track explores verified inference and is explicitly toy-scale.
  • The worlds are small. Cartpole and the watchdog habitat are deliberately tiny model instances. They demonstrate the architecture; they are not industrial control systems.

Receipts

Don't take our word. Here are the actual theorem statements and the actual transcript.

The headline theorem (HOL4, safetyScript.sml)

Theorem safety_preservation:
  init_safe init safe ∧
  sound_policy step safe pol ∧
  safe_shield step safe shield ⇒
  ∀ctrl. invariant step init (enveloped pol shield ctrl) safe
Proof
  rpt strip_tac >>
  irule invariant_intro >>
  metis_tac[enveloped_step_closed]
QED

For every controller (∀ctrl) the enveloped system keeps the safety invariant. The controller is never constrained.

Self-improvement cannot lose safety (upgradeScript.sml)

Theorem self_improvement_is_safe:
  init_safe init safe ∧
  safe_shield step safe shield ∧
  sound_policy step safe p0 ⇒
  ∀proposals ctrl.
    invariant step init
      (enveloped (admit_all step safe p0 proposals) shield ctrl) safe
QED

No finite stream of self-proposed envelope weakenings — adversarial or not — can make the system unsafe, for any controller.

Kernel self-upgrade, with the assumption visible (kernelUpgradeScript.sml)

Theorem self_improving_kernel_is_safe:
  is_set_theory ^mem ∧
  loeb_reflection ^mem candle_kernel K' sound_stmt ∧   (* labelled assumption *)
  (∀thy. candle_kernel thy (sound_stmt thy)) ∧
  K' thy obl ∧
  encodes_obligation ^mem thy obl step safe oldp newp ∧ (* labelled assumption *)
  init_safe init safe ∧ safe_shield step safe shield ∧
  sound_policy step safe oldp ⇒
  ∀ctrl. invariant step init
            (enveloped (admit step safe oldp newp) shield ctrl) safe
QED

Proved outright from the built Candle soundness theorem plus the two explicit, labelled hypotheses. No cheat.

Certified live by the running Candle kernel (candle/theplace.ml)

let SAFETY_PRESERVATION = prove
 (`(!s. init s ==> safe s) /\
   (!s a. safe s /\ pol s a ==> safe (step s a)) /\
   (!s. safe s ==> safe (step s (shield s)))
   ==> !ctrl. invariant step init (enveloped pol shield ctrl) safe`,
  REWRITE_TAC[invariant] THEN STRIP_TAC THEN GEN_TAC THEN
  MATCH_MP_TAC reach_INDUCT THEN
  ASM_REWRITE_TAC[enveloped] THEN REPEAT STRIP_TAC THEN
  COND_CASES_TAC THEN ASM_MESON_TAC[]);;

The same theorem as the HOL4 core, re-derived in HOL Light and checked at runtime by the verified cake binary — the prover the architecture is about, verifying the architecture.
